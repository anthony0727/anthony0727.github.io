I read this book around age 13, and a part of the book talked about uncertainty princible, Heizenberg.

As a young kid, I understood nothing about quantum mechanics, but the idea that something probabilistically exist (sorry for the losse term), made me think about,

"What does it mean that something exists?", "Should it be physically present? tangible?", 

<img src="/assets/what-is-existence/image1.png" width="40%" />

"Or does it exist only when I admit that it exists?"

"Then if the physical existence of a matter is a 'realization' of some supernatural function, then should I look beyond my human perception?"

"But isn't human perception bounded by brain system?" 

Pondering on these questions, I came up with this funny idea,

"Saying something 'doesn't exist' is false statement — because non-existence already implies that it exists as a concept."
